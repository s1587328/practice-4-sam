Piet Mondrian

Piet was a Dutch painter and pioneer of abstract art and was the cofounder of the Dr Stijl movement.

The painting was an early example of neo-plasticism, a movement that sought to highlight artistic expression through geometric forms and primary colors.

1872 - Born in Amersfoort, Netherlands

1911 - Moved to Paris

1917 - Started developing his abstract style

1930 - Painted Composition with Red, Blue, and Yellow

1944 - Died in New York City

Behind the Composition with Red, Blue, and Yellow

Created in 1930 during Mondrian’s mature period in Paris, the painting reflects his engagement with the De Stijl movement, which he co-founded with Theo van Doesburg. De Stijl emphasized geometric abstraction, primary colors, and rectilinear forms as a universal visual language. Mondrian’s exposure to Cubism in Paris influenced his move toward abstraction, but he sought a more universal, spiritual expression through simplified forms and primary colors.

This is an early example of the geometric mode of painting that Mondrian called Neo-Plasticism. The abstract two-dimensional nature of these compositions formed a new universal aesthetic language that was popularized through the magazine De Stijl. The avant-garde movement known by the same name held the promise of constructing a postwar world with a common point of visual reference, a way of abolishing artistic and even social hierarchies.

Mondrian uses thick black lines to divide the canvas into eleven different rectangles, some of which are painted in primary shades of red and blue. He created lighter hues by mixing primary colors with white. Over time, Mondrian ceased diluting his palette altogether in favor of pure primary colors.
